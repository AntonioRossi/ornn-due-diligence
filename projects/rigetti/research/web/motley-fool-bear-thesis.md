# Motley Fool Bear Thesis — "Prediction: This Will Be Rigetti Computing's Stock Price in 1 Year"

Source: https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/05/19/prediction-this-will-be-rigetti-computings-stock-p/
Author: Leo Sun (Motley Fool)
Published: May 19, 2026
Scraped: 2026-05-27

## Core argument (verbatim)

> "Rigetti's stock has plunged more than 70% from its record high."
> "It still looks expensive relative to its near-term growth potential."
> "Therefore, I believe Rigetti's stock will either stagnate or sink over the next 12 months as it struggles to justify its sky-high valuations."

## Historical revenue trajectory (per Motley Fool data, sourced from MarketScreener)

| Year | Revenue | Net Loss |
|------|---------|----------|
| 2022 | $13.1M | $(71.5)M |
| 2023 | $12.0M | $(75.1)M |
| 2024 | $10.8M | $(201.0)M |
| 2025 | $7.1M | $(216.2)M |

**Revenue has declined every year since 2022.** Q1 2026 result of $4.4M is the first inflection.

## Forward analyst projections (per Motley Fool)

- 2026E revenue: $23.6M (+232% from 2025)
- 2027E revenue: $44M (+86% from 2026)

## Valuation math (per Motley Fool, using $5.5B market cap reference)

> "But with a market cap of $5.5 billion, Rigetti still looks expensive at **126 times its 2027 sales**."

Note: market cap actually $8.33B at scrape date (May 26, 2026), which would put forward P/S at ~189× 2027E sales.

## Competitive concerns acknowledged

- Stiff competition from IonQ and IBM
- Bull case requires Novera shipments resuming (stopped 2025, resumed 2026)
- Cepheus systems scaling
- QCS platform expansion

## Lumpy revenue framing

> "Over the past few years, Rigetti's revenue growth has been lumpy with widening net losses. That's because its growth was driven by an uneven mix of government and research contracts, occasional Novera shipments, and occasional use of its QCS platform rather than predictable, recurring revenues."

## Conclusion

Motley Fool author Leo Sun forecasts stagnation or decline over the next 12 months.

## Investor takeaways

- Motley Fool's bear thesis is **structurally consistent**: valuation expanded faster than commercial traction
- 70% pullback from $58 ATH to ~$17 (at article time; now ~$25) creates some valuation cushion but not enough at 189× forward 2027 sales
- The most damning data point: **revenue has declined 4 years in a row** (FY2022 → FY2025). Q1 2026 inflection is necessary but not yet sufficient to validate the growth narrative.
- Lumpy revenue dependency on government and research contracts ≠ scalable enterprise SaaS metrics
