# ByteTree Token Takeaway: Kaspa (Aug 2024)

Source: https://www.bytetree.com/research/2024/08/beyond-bitcoin-kaspas-ascent-in-the-proof-of-work-realm/
Author: Shehriyar Ali, ByteTree
Published: August 29, 2024
Scraped: 2026-05-27

## Positioning

- Kaspa as next-generation PoW L1 addressing scalability of Bitcoin
- "Doesn't aim to outshine or replace Bitcoin — that would be impossible"
- Frames Kaspa as alternative for users; bitcoin evolves into store of value, Kaspa for peer-to-peer transactions
- "Core principles of both projects are quite similar"

## Funding history (verbatim)

> "Kaspa was developed by DAGLabs, a blockchain research and development company led by Yonatan Sompolinsky. Polychain Capital primarily funded the project following an initial $8m funding round as a vote of confidence. DAGLabs repaid Polychain Capital by using some of the initially mined KAS tokens. According to Kaspa Wiki, DAGLabs and Polychain Capital have both waived all Kaspa intellectual property rights, officially making Kaspa a "community-owned" project."

## Key fact

- DAGLabs and Polychain Capital have **both formally waived all Kaspa intellectual property rights**
- This is the legal mechanism by which Kaspa became "community-owned"
- DAGLabs initially supported Kaspa developers at half-capacity, then dissolved

## ByteTree investment framing

- Considers Kaspa a legitimate L1 with sound architectural foundations
- Bitcoin transaction fee spikes (940% in single day to $7.60 on Aug 22, 2024) cited as recurring scalability evidence
- Kaspa positioned as solution to the blockchain trilemma

## Investor takeaways

- ByteTree is a respected independent institutional research firm (London-based) - its inclusion of Kaspa in Token Takeaway series confers some institutional credibility
- The explicit IP rights waiver claim (verified via Kaspa Wiki) is the strongest legal argument for "no lingering VC equity" - cite in IC memo
- This research dates from August 2024, before Crescendo and Toccata announcements - some technical claims (1 BPS) are outdated
